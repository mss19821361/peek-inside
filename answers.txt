# 2TR8
Great question, Matin! Your hands have two kinds of lines.

The big lines on your palm are folding lines. Your skin there is a bit like paper that has been folded many times. When you close your hand, the skin folds along those lines, so you can make a fist and hold things tightly. Try it: close your hand slowly and watch the lines fold!

The tiny swirly lines on your fingertips are your fingerprints. They work a bit like the bumps on the bottom of a shoe. They help your fingers grip things so they don't slip, and they help you feel things better.

Wow fact: your hand lines started growing before you were even born, while you were still in Mummy's tummy!

So why is everybody's fingerprint different? When a baby is growing, the skin on the fingertips grows a little bit faster underneath, so it wrinkles up into tiny ridges, like a rug that gets wrinkly when you push it. But exactly how it wrinkles depends on lots of tiny things: how fast each finger grows, how the baby is lying, and even the baby touching the soft walls of the tummy. All those little things are a bit different for every baby. That's why nobody in the whole world has the same fingerprints as you, not even twins! It is like your own secret code, and that is why phones and passports can use fingerprints to know it's really you.
