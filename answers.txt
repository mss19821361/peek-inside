# 2TR8
Great question, Matin! Your hands have two kinds of lines.

The big lines on your palm are folding lines. Your skin there is a bit like paper that has been folded many times. When you close your hand, the skin folds along those lines, so you can make a fist and hold things tightly. Try it: close your hand slowly and watch the lines fold!

The tiny swirly lines on your fingertips are your fingerprints. They work a bit like the bumps on the bottom of a shoe. They help your fingers grip things so they don't slip, and they help you feel things better.

Wow fact: your hand lines started growing before you were even born, while you were still in Mummy's tummy!

So why is everybody's fingerprint different? When a baby is growing, the skin on the fingertips grows a little bit faster underneath, so it wrinkles up into tiny ridges, like a rug that gets wrinkly when you push it. But exactly how it wrinkles depends on lots of tiny things: how fast each finger grows, how the baby is lying, and even the baby touching the soft walls of the tummy. All those little things are a bit different for every baby. That's why nobody in the whole world has the same fingerprints as you, not even twins! It is like your own secret code, and that is why phones and passports can use fingerprints to know it's really you.

Airplanes cost a LOT of money! A big passenger plane, like the ones at our airport, costs about 120 million dollars. That is about as much as 4,000 cars!

A giant plane that flies all the way across the ocean can cost more than 300 million dollars.

A tiny plane with just 4 seats, the kind people learn to fly in, costs about half a million dollars. That is about the same as a house!

Airlines often buy lots of planes at once, so they get a big discount, a bit like when you buy a big bag of sweets instead of one at a time.

# XCZ9
What a clever question, Matin! Lots of ships have no cabins at all. A rowing boat, a canoe or a little sailing dinghy has none, because nobody sleeps on board. You just go home at the end of the day!

Big working ships have only a few cabins. A huge container ship can be longer than three football pitches, but it has only about 20 to 30 cabins, one for each sailor in the crew. All the rest of the ship is space for containers.

Cruise ships are the opposite: they are built for sleeping! The biggest cruise ship in the world, Icon of the Seas, has about 2,800 cabins. That's like a floating town.

So the ship with the fewest cabins is a small boat with zero, and the biggest cruise ships have thousands!

# WNE3
Great question, Matin! Almost every big cruise ship that sails across the sea has a water maker. Out in the middle of the ocean there are no taps, so the ship has to turn salty sea water into fresh water. A big cruise ship can make enough fresh water every day to fill several swimming pools!

But some cruise ships have no water maker at all. River cruise ships, the long, flat boats that sail along big rivers, stop in a new town almost every day. At the dock they connect a big hose and fill their tanks with fresh water, just like filling a giant water bottle!

Long ago, before water makers were invented, sailing ships carried all their drinking water in wooden barrels. Sailors had to be careful not to waste a drop, because they could not get more until they reached land.

So ships far out at sea need water makers, and ships that are close to towns can just fill up at the dock.
